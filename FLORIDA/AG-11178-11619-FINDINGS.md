# The Baselines That Are Not There

*Prepared September 5, 2026, on Auditor General Reports No. 11178 (March 1, 1989) and No. 11619 (April 23, 1991), supplied by the Auditor General's office September 5, 2026, OCR'd locally by `ocr_pdf_v1.sh` at 300 dpi. Companion to `AG-12581-13043-FINDINGS.md` and `AG-12061-FINDINGS.md`. Full extractions in `_working/AG-11178-extraction.md` and `_working/AG-11619-extraction.md`, produced September 5, 2026 by Claude Opus 5 subagents.*

The archive requested these two reports on the strength of Report 12581's Finding #9, which cites both for the Bureau of Vital Statistics death-record comparison finding, and on Kastner's characterization: 11178 as the pre-FLORIDA operational baseline, 11619 as the transition-era baseline. The reports turn out to be less than that, and in one specific way, differently than that. The point of this note is to say plainly what they are and what they are not, so the archive does not carry a wrong belief about either.

---

## 1. Neither report is a systems audit

**11178** is a management-controls audit. Its ¶3 lists the ten control areas examined; **data processing is not among them.** The Department's own response is captioned "AUDIT OF ADMINISTRATIVE COMPLIANCE AND MANAGEMENT CONTROLS." Data-processing content survives only where a cash-control finding happened to route through a computer.

**11619** is narrower still: its ¶3 limits the scope to institutional fees, client trust funds, and an Office of State Ombudsman follow-up. Full-text search confirms zero occurrences of FLORIDA System, EDS, IBM, RFP, Advance Planning Document, DHHS, FNS, ACF, Food Stamp quality control, AFDC caseloads, or any description of an eligibility system. The audit period straddles the RFP release, the EDS award, and the contract signing — and the report is silent on all three.

**What this means for the archive's baseline argument.** The "new system worse than old system" comparison Kastner set up cannot be built from these two reports alone. Neither carries the pre-FLORIDA operational metrics — caseload, Food Stamp error rate, timeliness, response time, availability — the comparison would need on the before side. The metrics exist somewhere in the audit record; they are not here.

---

## 2. What 11178 does supply

Three items worth having, none load-bearing.

**MORS described in operational terms.** The Money Order Recipient System was the pre-FLORIDA payment mechanism. ¶¶44, 45, 49 give its architecture in three paragraphs: paper HRS Form 2012 → centralized keypunching at the Jacksonville Data Center → monthly computer tape → Comptroller prints warrants → payroll-by-exception recurrence. The auditor's own sentence: *"the MORS does not generate a turnaround document"* [¶49]. That is the pre-FLORIDA architecture from an auditor, in the Department's window, in written form — an actual baseline against which FLORIDA's real-time online-eligibility promises can be measured, even if only qualitatively.

**Economic Services staffing at June 30, 1987: 6,487.69 filled of 6,828.85 authorized FTE** [¶18]. Report 12061's Finding #7 Management Response cited "162.5% caseload growth from 1987–1992" against only "3.7%" staffing growth in the public assistance programs. This gives the 1987 denominator. If Economic Services staffing in 1992 was roughly 6,724 (6,487.69 × 1.037), the archive can test the 3.7% claim against actual figures if it obtains a 1992 headcount. The check is worth doing before the 3.7% figure appears in any published paper.

**The FSA-1988 pool commitment through October 1990** [Exhibit D, ¶¶47–54]. One Management Systems pool served both MORS and the Assistance Payment System, fully committed to Family Support Act of 1988 changes through October 1990 — inside the FLORIDA build period, which began at EDS in June 1989. Whether the same programming pool was expected to support FLORIDA is not stated. If yes, it explains why implementation slid; if no, it means HRS was running two large concurrent programming efforts on separate staff — a fact the archive should establish and hold.

## 3. What 11619 does supply

Also three items.

**The pre-FLORIDA software's age is documented.** Department response at Exhibit E, ¶¶74–77: *"The software package was purchased in 1976 and has been extensively modified periodically to maintain the current service level,"* and *"We believe the only accurate record keeping that can be done is outside the system."* The software HRS was running in 1990 was fourteen years old and the Department itself said accurate record-keeping had to happen outside it. That is the strongest statement in the two reports of what FLORIDA was replacing and why replacement was reasonable on its face, regardless of how it turned out.

**Income Verification was a paper batch loop.** Exhibit E ¶¶54–57: IVS requests *"prepared manually … batched and forwarded to the HRS Technology Center for keypunching. Responses are returned … in the form of computer printouts which are separated and mailed."* This is the third confirmed pre-FLORIDA architectural pattern — paper request, batch keypunch, printout return, mail delivery. The archive can now describe the pre-FLORIDA environment as consistently batch-oriented with paper interfaces at both ends.

**MORS payroll-by-exception failure at scale, documented.** ¶124: 771 cases, 4,957 warrants, approximately $129,000 paid after death; 79 cases still generating warrants as of November 1989. This is the direct 11619 predecessor to 12061's ¶¶74–77 death-record finding (12061 Finding #9), and it is what 12581 later inherited.

**And the promise that was not kept.** ¶128 records the Department's stated commitment: *"Department personnel stated on July 20, 1990, that a quarterly computer match … would be instituted"* — the BVS death-record match. First recommended in 11178 (1989). Promised in 1990 for quarterly implementation. Still open in 12061 (1993). Still open in 12581 (1995). Fifteen years across four audit cycles. That is a documented commitment-and-failure sequence, and it is worth quoting in the archive not for FLORIDA-specific reasons but because it establishes the Department's baseline behavior on audit commitments, which is context every reader of the FLORIDA settlement should have.

**One non-concurrence to note.** ¶¶106–110, the client-fund investment finding — the only non-concurrence in either report. The Department pushed back on the auditor. Not a FLORIDA matter; a note on Department disposition patterns.

## 4. The BVS citation problem

Report 12061's Finding #9 (¶¶74–77) cites *both* Report 11178 and Report 11619 for the BVS death-record comparison finding. The full extractions confirm:

- **11619 ¶128** does contain the BVS finding and the July 20, 1990 quarterly-match commitment. The citation is correct.
- **11178 contains no BVS reference at all.** The Opus extraction searched the full document and flagged this absence explicitly.

Two possible explanations. Either 12061's citation to 11178 for BVS is wrong, or it is pointing to a *different* report number that got OCR-garbled. Report 11178's own report number is OCR'd inconsistently as "11131738," "113178," and "11178" across its own pages — so a similarly garbled cross-reference in 12061 is plausible.

**Before the archive treats either finding as wrong, sight-check 12061's Finding #9 in the searchable PDF.** If 12061 really does cite 11178 for BVS, the archive has to note that 11178 does not contain what it is cited for. If 12061 cites a different number and the OCR garbled it, we chase the correct number.

## 6. The "new system worse than old system" argument, honestly stated

Given what the reports do and do not contain, here is what the archive can and cannot say.

**Can say:**

- The pre-FLORIDA environment was batch-oriented with paper interfaces, running a 1976 software package the Department itself said required record-keeping outside it.
- MORS was payment-by-exception, with a documented failure mode at scale ($129,000 posthumous payments, 771 cases, ¶124).
- The Department promised the BVS quarterly match in 1990 and failed to deliver it across four subsequent audits, which places FLORIDA's own delivery failures within a longer institutional pattern rather than as an isolated event.
- The 1987 Economic Services staffing figure is documented (6,487.69 filled), so the 12061 Department claim of 3.7% staffing growth 1987–92 is now testable against evidence rather than accepted on the Department's word.

**Cannot say from these two reports:**

- What the pre-FLORIDA Food Stamp QC error rate was.
- What pre-FLORIDA application-processing timeliness looked like.
- What the pre-FLORIDA caseload was.
- What the pre-FLORIDA transaction volume was.
- How the pre-FLORIDA system performed on response time or availability.

Those figures are the ones a "new system worse than old system" argument would rest on. They are not in 11178 or 11619. They may be in 10983, in the annual federal financial assistance audits (12363 FYE 6/30/93 and 12565 FYE 6/30/94, both already on the collection list), or in HRS annual reports and DHHS/FNS quality control reports from the era.

**The archive's honest position is:** the pre-FLORIDA baseline is thinner than it hoped. Two of the three reports the July request identified as pre-FLORIDA baselines turn out to be scoped elsewhere. The system was old and the Department knew it; whether it worked better or worse than what replaced it on measured operational dimensions cannot be answered from what we have. Kastner's "common, avoidable, and ironic" observation about new-system-worse-than-old is a general truth about IT modernization and is worth stating; whether it applied to FLORIDA is a claim the archive cannot yet make.
