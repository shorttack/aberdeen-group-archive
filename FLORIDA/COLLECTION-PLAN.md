## Why this file exists

On August 19, 2026 the archive completed a full mirroring pass over every load-bearing source cited in its papers: 99 of 105 now have local full-text copies under `sources/`, and the single remaining gap is a generic IBM glossary page of no evidentiary weight (`SOURCES.md`, "Mirroring pass, August 19, 2026").

That pass exhausted the open web. Everything the archive still needs falls into one of three categories that no retrieval tool can reach:

1. **Documents that were never digitized** and exist only as paper or microfilm in Tallahassee.
2. **Documents behind a paid or institutional credential** — Westlaw, NewsBank, ProQuest, a federal depository library.
3. **Testimony that exists only in living memory**, held by people who are now in their seventies and eighties.

The third category is the only one with an expiration date, and it is listed last only because the first two inform what questions to ask.

## Section 0 — The retention problem, and why it changes the plan

**Read this before booking anything.** This section is the most consequential in the file, and it is largely *inference* from the applicable rules rather than a confirmed fact about these specific case files. It is labeled as such throughout.

The archive has been assuming that the Leon County circuit court files are the most likely surviving home of RFP 88-74-BC, on the reasoning that "the RFP was certainly an exhibit in 1992 CA 003618" (`FLORIDA-system-addendum-sources.md`). That assumption needs to be qualified before a trip is planned around it, because Florida's judicial retention rule is far shorter than the archive has been treating it.

Under [Florida Rule of General Practice and Judicial Administration 2.430](https://www.courtrules.net/florida/florida-general-practice/rule-2-430), the retention schedule for trial courts sets **five years** for "civil proceedings in circuit court except marriage dissolutions and adoptions," running from the date judgment became final. Separately, [Fla. Stat. § 28.213](https://cdn.ymaws.com/www.flclerks.com/resource/resmgr/Presentations/Records-Evidence_Retention_a.pdf) permits the clerk to dispose of physical evidence held as exhibits in excess of **three years** where no appeal is pending, and Rule 2.430 requires civil exhibits to be retained only until 90 days after judgment becomes final, after which unclaimed exhibits may be disposed of.

**Inference (high confidence):** the paper case files for 1992 CA 003618 and 1995 CA 005280 passed their minimum retention thresholds around the turn of the century, and the *exhibits* — the category that would contain the RFP — passed theirs even earlier. The archive should stop describing the Leon County exhibit file as a likely custodian and start describing it as a long shot.

**Three things survive this, and they are the reason to go anyway.**

First, Rule 2.430(d) requires **permanent** retention of "progress dockets, and other similar records generated to document activity in a case." The docket is not the file. But a 1992–1997 circuit civil docket in a case of this size will list, by date and title, every motion, order, deposition, and filed exhibit. **Inference (high confidence):** the docket is a map of what documents once existed, who filed them, and which law firms held copies — which is precisely the information needed to pursue the same documents through non-court custodians who have no five-year clock.

Second, the rule permits destruction of records that have been "permanently recorded," defined as microfilmed, optically imaged, or recorded to an electronic system meeting Supreme Court standards ([In re Amendments to Rule 2.430](https://law.justia.com/cases/florida/supreme-court/2008/sc06-2040.html)). **Inference (moderate confidence):** where a clerk destroyed paper *after* imaging it, the image survives even though the paper does not. Whether Leon County imaged early-1990s circuit civil files before disposal is an empirical question that one phone call answers, and it is the single highest-value pre-trip call in this plan.

Third, retention periods are **minimums, not mandates**. Records custodians may retain longer, and the general schedules say so explicitly ([GS11 for Clerks of Court](https://files.floridados.gov/media/706725/gs11-june-2023.pdf)). Large, notorious, politically sensitive cases sometimes survive on precisely that discretion.

**Consequence for planning.** The trip should be reweighted away from the courthouse and toward the two custodians with no five-year clock: the **State Archives** (which appraises state-agency records for permanent historical retention) and the **State Library** (whose newspaper microfilm is permanent by nature). The courthouse becomes a half-day docket-reconnaissance stop whose product is a target list, not the documents themselves.

## Section 4 — The Tallahassee trip

### 4.1 Trip thesis

**Go for the State Archives and the State Library. Treat the courthouse as reconnaissance.** Section 0 is the reason. The two R.A. Gray Building institutions hold materials with permanent or indefinite retention; the courthouse holds a permanent docket and, probably, not much else. Structuring the trip the other way around risks spending the best hours of the visit confirming a destruction.

Savannah to Tallahassee is roughly 250 miles, about four hours by car (approximate — verify routing). Two full working days on the ground is the right size: three is padding, one guarantees you leave with loose ends and no time to chase them.

*1:30–4:00, DCF (2415 N. Monroe) or return to the Gray Building.* Decide based on whether the T-4-week DCF request produced a live contact. **In-person follow-up on an existing request is materially more productive than initiating one cold** — you are giving a named person a face and a specific ask rather than adding a ticket to a queue. ### 4.5 Field kit and capture protocol

**Bring:** camera or phone with **copystand or small tripod**, spare batteries and a power bank, laptop, portable scanner if you own one, white cotton gloves (some archives require them; most no longer do, but bring them), pencils only — **most archives prohibit pens in reading rooms** — printed copies of the archive's open-items lists, printed copies of the sent records requests, photo ID, and cash or card for fees.

**Capture rules, so material drops cleanly into `archival-ingest`:**

- Photograph, do not photocopy. It is faster, free, higher-resolution, and preserves layout. Confirm each institution's photography policy on arrival; all three routinely permit it for personal research.
- **Shoot the folder, box, and series label before shooting contents.** Provenance captured at the moment of capture is provenance you will still have in six months. Retrofitting it is the single most common archival research failure.
- One folder per subject, named `YYYY-MM-DD_institution_series_folder`.
- Keep a running capture log with box/folder/series identifiers, so every image can be cited to a specific holding rather than to "the State Archives."
- Photograph **finding aids and indexes**, not just documents. They tell you what you did not have time to pull.
- Back up to two locations before leaving Tallahassee. A single trip's images are irreplaceable in a way the trip itself is not.

**On ingestion:** each acquisition becomes an `archival-ingest` study package, and mirrored copies belong in `sources/` under the same header standard used in the August 19 mirroring pass — source, URL or physical citation, title, author, date, retrieval date, citing files, and the specific claim it bears on. For physical documents, substitute a full archival citation for the URL field.

## Section 6 — Assumptions and limits of this plan

Stated explicitly, per the archive's standing practice.

1. **Section 0 is inference, not verified fact about these files.** It applies the general retention rules to two specific cases without having confirmed their actual disposition. The T-3-week call to the Clerk exists precisely to test it. If the call shows the files intact, the trip should be re-weighted back toward the courthouse and this section should be corrected in place rather than quietly dropped.
2. **Addresses and hours were verified on August 19, 2026** against official sources and are cited above. Government hours change; reconfirm at T-1 week. Note that at least one widely-circulated genealogy guide still lists the Archives on the first floor with Saturday hours — the official Department of State page gives **second floor, Monday–Friday only**, and the official page governs.
3. **The mileage and drive time are approximate** and were not independently verified.
4. **The Tallahassee State College photo-collection suggestion is speculative.** No evidence has been seen that it contains HRS or data-center imagery; it is included because the cost of asking is one phone call.
5. **This plan does not re-run anything already in flight.** The Leon County grand-jury request (sent August 20, 2026, Clerk reference P022996-082026), the DCF ITN 03F12GC1 request, and the Carlton Fields request are pending (`FLORIDA-records-requests-drafts.md`). The DCF procurement-file request was answered August 19, 2026 with no responsive records — see § 1.1.
6. **One loose end on the outbound requests themselves.** `FLORIDA-records-requests-drafts.md` concedes that it does not record whether the §§ 3 and 4 amendments were incorporated before those requests were sent on July 28, 2026. That should be resolved from the sent copies before any follow-up, because an unamended § 4 returning "no responsive electronic records" would be an artifact of scoping rather than a finding about the records. Resolving it costs one look in a sent-mail folder and prevents misreading whatever comes back.

## Section 7 — Open research questions

Not acquisition targets — research questions the evidence has raised that need an answer before the paper can use them.

1. **The FSA-1988 programming pool overlap.** Report 11178 Exhibit D ¶¶47–54 records that one Management Systems programming pool served both MORS and the Assistance Payment System and was fully committed to Family Support Act of 1988 changes **through October 1990** — inside the FLORIDA build period (EDS development began June 1989). Whether the same pool was expected to support FLORIDA is not stated. If yes, it partly explains implementation slippage; if no, HRS was running two large concurrent programming efforts on separate staff. Establish which. Sources: HRS annual reports of the era, personnel-schedule appendices in the audits, oral history.
