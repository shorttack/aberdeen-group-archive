# DOAH Response — September 6, 2026

*Two documents from the Division of Administrative Hearings in response to Kastner's records request touching DOAH Case 88-002942 (Unisys Corporation v. Florida Department of Health and Rehabilitative Services), the bid protest of the FLORIDA System award to EDS. OCR'd locally September 6, 2026 by `ocr_pdf_v1.sh`.*

## The short version

**DOAH has nothing left of substantive value on Case 88-002942.**

The two documents supplied are:

1. **`88002942Docket.pdf`** (2 pages) — a fragmentary OCR of what appears to be the docket sheet for Case 88-002942. Filings from June and July 1988 are legible only in fragments; the OCR is heavily degraded. Enough survives to confirm the case identity — "Unisys' Memorandum of…" and "Unisys Corporation's Not[ice]…" appear on Page 2 — but no substantive filing text, no orders, no recommended order, no final order. Just the docket line-items.

2. **`Record_Disposition_Documents.pdf`** (12 pages) — DOAH's own routine records-management destruction authorizations from 1998, applying to generic categories ("Supplementary file material," "Correspondence (Routine) Copies") on standard state retention schedules. Zero references to Unisys, HRS, EDS, or the FLORIDA System. This is DOAH's answer to "did you destroy anything responsive to my request" — the answer being that they applied their standard destruction schedule and the case file no longer exists in its pre-1998 form.

## What that establishes

The Unisys protest file at DOAH has been substantially destroyed under routine records retention, in or around 1998, ten years after the case. What remains is a docket sheet with no attached filings.

## Implications for the collection plan

- **The Unisys bid protest transcript, briefs, and exhibits are not recoverable through DOAH.** The archive should not send follow-up requests to DOAH for the underlying pleadings.
- **Other custodians who may hold copies:** counsel of record for Unisys (identify from the docket if the OCR is sight-readable in the PDF; likely a Tallahassee firm as of 1988), counsel of record for HRS (Office of the General Counsel, HRS), the Auditor General's workpapers for Report 12061 or predecessor 10983 if they cited protest materials, and any Unisys corporate records archive if such a thing survived Unisys → Sperry → Unisys merger history and eventual DXC absorption.
- **This changes what the archive can plausibly obtain about the losing bid.** The "what did Unisys actually propose" question, which surfaced in yesterday's session close, is now materially harder to answer from public records.

## What is worth preserving from these documents anyway

The Records Disposition Document, though it names nothing FLORIDA-specific, is itself the documentary evidence that the file was destroyed. That is worth keeping as the proof of destruction for the archive's SOURCES file, so no future reader wonders why DOAH pleadings are missing.
