SCOPE AND OBJECTIVES

Compliance and operational performance audits are made
by the Auditor General as part of the Legislature's oversight
responsibility for operations of State agencies. Our audit, which
was made in accordance with generally accepted governmental
auditing standards, included examinations of various transactions
during the period July 1, 1986, through December 31, 1987, to
determine whether they were executed, both in manner and
substance, in accordance with governing provisions of law and
administrative rules. The proper administration of public funds
requires the adoption and use of procedures that will promote the
economic and efficient conduct of duties and responsibilities.
Consequently, an evaluation of selected administrative and
accounting controls was also included within the scope of this
audit.

Our examination of State agencies is accomplished
through a multiphase approach. As described in paragraphs 4 and
5, financial resources and controls over financial resources are
examined in our audit of the general purpose financial statements
of the State of Florida, and Federal resources received by State
agencies (of which the Department received in excess of $1.3
billion during the 1986-87 fiscal year) are examined in our
Statewide audit of Federal assistance received by the State of
Florida. Additionally, we perform compliance and operational
performance audits of selected functions and programs of the
various State agencies.

The activities of the Department of Health and
Rehabilitative Services cover a broad range of functions and
programs and involve thousands of employees located in offices
throughout the State. Because of the nature and diversity of the
Department's functions and programs, we examined selected
management control systems at the following representative
locations during this audit:

BACKGROUND

The Florida Department of Health and Rehabilitative

Services was created by Section 20.19, Florida Statutes, to
provide social, rehabilitative, and health-related services to
eligible clients in need of assistance. During the audit period,
services were provided to clients through one or more of the
following programs:

Children's Medical Services - provides medical care services
to children with physical conditions which may hinder their
normal growth and development.

Economic Services - administers all income-support programs
within the Department and receives and distributes food
stamps and food commodities received from the Federal
Government.

Developmental Services - prevents and reduces the severity of
mental retardation and other developmental disabilities and
provides treatment to clients with these disorders.

Aging and Adult Services - advises, assists, and protects the
elderly and other adult clients who are unable to perform the
normal tasks of daily living for themselves.

Children, Youth, and Families - includes adoption, child
care, and foster care programs; specialized services to
families; all programs related to delinquent children;
related mental health services for children and youth in
coordination with the Alcohol, Drug Abuse, and Mental Health
Services Program Office; and referrals for delinquency and
dependency juvenile programs.

Alcohol, Drug Abuse, and Mental Health - aids in the care,
treatment, and rehabilitation of alcoholics; provides
medical, psychological, and vocational rehabilitation
services to drug dependents; and is intended to reduce the
occurrence, severity, duration, and disabling effects of
mental, behavioral, and emotional disorders.

Vocational Rehabilitation Services - promotes the vocational
rehabilitation of physically disabled persons and thereby
enables them to return to employment. Effective October l,
1986, this Program was transferred to the Florida Department

FINDINGS AND RECOMMENDATIONS

Legal Compliance

We have examined the Department's compliance with
applicable laws and rules for those operations within the scope of
this audit. As a part of our audit, we performed such tests and
other auditing procedures as we considered necessary. Except as
noted under the subheading Management Controls, our examination
indicated that the Department had generally complied with the
various guidelines governing those operations. Except as
described in paragraphs 65 and 66, nothing came to our attention
during our examination that caused us to believe that untested
items were not substantially in compliance with applicable laws
and rules.

Management Controls

The proper administration of public funds requires
adoption and use of procedures by the Department that will promote
the economical and efficient conduct of assigned duties and
responsibilities. Management is responsible for establishing and
maintaining the management controls necessary for achieving the
objectives of the organization. These management controls should
also provide reasonable assurance of the reliability of financial
records and provide for the safeguarding of assets. We have
examined the Department's management systems intended to control
those operations within the scope of this audit as discussed in
paragraph 3. Except as noted in the following paragraphs, nothing
came to our attention to indicate that those systems reviewed were
inadequate.

Revenues and Cash Receipts

An adequate system of internal control over the cash
receipts function must be established to promote the safeguarding
of assets and the accuracy of accounting records. Physical
controls over assets and accounting records are an essential
feature of an effective system of internal accounting control.
Physical controls are necessary to safeguard certain assets and
prevent unauthorized access or use. Such a system should include
consideration of documentation and authorization procedures,
segregation of duties among employees, and independent internal
verification procedures.

Documentation procedures used to achieve such controls
include restrictive endorsement of checks immediately upon receipt
and the preparation of records evidencing the collection of moneys
immediately upon receipt, for example, through the issuance of
prenumbered receipts or through the use of a receipt log
(prelist). Restrictive endorsement at the point of receipt limits
the negotiability of checks should they be lost or diverted, while
recording moneys at the point of collection by use of prenumbered
receipts or logs facilitates accountability over receipts.
Authorization procedures ensure that transactions are authorized
by management personnel acting within the scope of their
authority, with the record of such authorization (documentation)
serving to establish accountability for actions taken.
Additionally, an adequate system of internal control should: (1)
direct that moneys be remitted to an area other than the area that
initiated the billing process; (2) safeguard the moneys until they
are properly deposited; and (3) provide for a reconciliation
between the amounts received and deposited to ensure that all
moneys were deposited intact.

In order for the controls noted above to function
properly, there must be an adequate segregation of duties in those
areas responsible for cash receipts activities. Certain duties
should be divided among employees and organizational areas so that
no one employee will be in a position to complete a transaction
which has not been reviewed, approved, or recorded by other
employees or units. For example, the individual who has access to
cash receipts generally should not have access to the accounting
records or reconcile the account unless there are compensating
controls. A proper segregation of duties in an organization,
accompanied by proper documentation procedures, provides an
environment for the prompt detection of errors and/or
irregularities which assists in safeguarding assets and ensuring
the accuracy of records.

Independent internal verification determines whether
other elements in the system are working as intended and includes
control features such as monitoring, recalculations, and
comparison of recorded accountability with assets and other
records. For this control element to be effective, it must not be
performed by the employee responsible for the information or
assets.

Deposits Into the State Treasury

®

Section 116.01(1), Florida Statutes, provides, in part, that
every State officer authorized to collect funds due the State
shall pay all sums officially received by the officer into
the State Treasury not later than seven working days from the
close of the week in which the officer received the funds.
Our review of the Department's Office of Licensure and
Certification (OLC) disclosed that 4 of 58 randomly selected
receipts (approximately 7 percent) tested were not deposited
in accordance with Section 116.01(1), Florida Statutes.
During other testing at the OLC, we noted an additional 7 of
30 judgmentally selected receipts (approximately 23 percent)
were not deposited in accordance with the above-noted law.

An additional 26 instances of noncompliance were noted during
our review of documentation (mail logs) applicable to the
initial 11 instances of noncompliance. These 37 receipts,
which were deposited from 1 to 23 working days late, totaled
$56,894.32. Total deposits made during the 1986-87 fiscal
year by the Licensure Section were approximately $2,212,000.
The untimely deposits generally resulted from funds being
held an excessive amount of time (6 to 26 working days) by
employees in the Licensure Section before being transferred
to an employee in the Administrative Services Section of the
Deputy Assistant Secretary for Regulation and Health
Facilities who made the deposits. The untimely deposit of
collections increases the risk of loss or misappropriation
and reduces the interest that would be earned had the moneys
been deposited in a more timely manner.

Section 18.101, Florida Statutes, requires that moneys
deposited into clearing funds should be transmitted to the

Department established written procedures (HRS 11 APM 1) that
require compliance with this law and administrative
regulation. The Department established the Licenses and Fees
Clearing Account (Special Account-2) for the deposit of
collections received by various Departmental units (including
the OLC) from the issuance/assessment of permits, licenses,
and fines. Our examination of the 25 deposits made into the
