AUDIT REPORT SUMMARY

This audit

report summary highlights the scope, objectives,

methodology, and findings of audit report No. 11619, dated April 23, 1991,
1991. It is intended to present the general findings of our report in a

condensed fashion.

The entire audit report should be read for a

comprehensive understanding of our audit findings.

SCOPE

Compliance and operational performance audits are made
by the Auditor General as part of the Legislature's
oversight responsibility for operations of State agencies.
The proper administration of public funds requires that
management establish and maintain an internal control
structure, including the control environment, accounting
system, and policies and procedures, to _ provide
reasonable assurance that specific entity objectives will

be achieved.

Our audit of the Florida Department of Health and
Rehabilitative Services included:

- Examinations of various transactions and Department
actions during the period July 1, 1988, through
March 31, 1990, and selected Department actions
taken through June 30, 1990, to determine whether
they were executed, both in manner and substance,
in accordance with governing provisions of laws,
administrative rules and regulations, and grantor

restrictions.

OBJECTIVES

An evaluation of selected control environment
factors, accounting system methods and records,
and policies and procedures of the Department's
internal control structure applicable to those
programs, activities, and functions and classes of
transactions within the scope of audit.

We conducted our audit in accordance with generally
accepted auditing standards and GOVERNMENT AUDITING
STANDARDS, issued by the Comptroller General of the
United States.

The Department administers numerous programs,
activities, and functions pursuant to governing provisions
of laws, administrative rules and regulations, and grantor
restrictions. Additionally, Department management has
established accounting systems and policies and
procedures for the various. classes of transactions
necessary to its operations. The nature and diversity of
the Department's operations preclude an examination of all
programs, activities, and functions and classes of
transactions during each audit. Accordingly, the scope
of this audit was limited to the internal control structure
and classes of transactions, at representative locations,
relative to fees assessed individuals and/or appropriate
entities for services rendered to clients of the
Department's institutions and facilities; personal funds
administered by the Department on behalf of the clients
residing in the Department's institutions and facilities;
and benefits paid to clients through the Optional State
Supplementation Program.

Our audit objectives for the programs, activities, and

functions and classes of transactions noted above were:

To evaluate the Department's performance in

Fees for Client Services

During the 1988-89 fiscal year the Florida Department of
Health and Rehabilitative Services rendered services to

approximately 11,000 clients residing in the Department's
institutions and facilities. In accordance with Section
402.33, Florida Statutes, the Department assessed clients,
responsible parties, and liable third-parties fees for the
cost of services rendered those clients. Fees collected
during that period totaled $116,280,508. These fees were
collected at the various institutions and facilities as well
as Department Headquarters and district offices. The
Department established polices and procedures for the
safeguarding and_ depositing of fee collections,
establishment of billing rates, determination and
identification of liable third-parties and client earnings
subject to assessments, determination of
clients'/responsible parties' abilities to pay, billing
procedures, management reviews of assessed fees, steps
to be taken in regard to collecting delinquent accounts,

etc.

Our review disclosed that the Department had generally
complied with the established procedures. However, as
described below, we noted areas in which improvements
are needed to provide the Department assurance that fees
to which it is entitled are properly assessed, collected,
and deposited, and to provide adequate records to enable
Department management to effectively monitor and manage

the fee assessment/collection process.

- The internal control structure established for client
services could be improved to provide reasonable
assurance that fees collected for the cost of services
rendered to clients are properly deposited and/or
safeguarded. Specifically, duties were not always
adequately segregated among employees; records of
initial receipts were not always reconciled to
appropriate evidence of deposit by persons
independent of the collection and deposit process;

Client Trust Funds

In accordance with Section 402.17, Florida Statutes, the
Department administers personal funds held on behalf of
clients residing in the Department's institutions and
facilities. During the 1988-89 fiscal year, receipts of
client funds totaled $6,790,108, while disbursements
totaled $6,563,746. The Department established policies
and procedures for’ the safeguarding, accounting,
depositing, disposition, and administration of client trust
funds. Our review disclosed that client trust funds were
generally administered in accordance with controlling
laws, administrative rules, and Department regulations.
However, as described below, we noted areas in which
established laws, rules, and/or regulations were not
always followed or adequate, and areas in which
improvements are needed to provide the Department
assurance that client trust funds are properly and
adequately safeguarded, deposited, accounted for,
disposed of, and invested.

Adequate controls were not always in place and/or
established procedures followed to provide
reasonable assurances that client trust funds were
properly deposited and safeguarded. Specifically,
records of initial collections were not always

Optional State Supplementation Program

In audit report No. 11178, dated ‘March 1, 1989,
paragraphs 47 through 54, we noted that independent
verifications were not performed to ensure that Optional
State Supplementation (OSS) Program benefit payments
were made only to authorized clients. Two instances
were cited in audit report No. 11178 in which OSS benefit
payments were made for periods ranging from 19 to 24
months subsequent to the deaths of the applicable
clients. In our follow-up of this deficiency, we compared
the Department's computerized records of OSS benefit
payments to the Department's computerized vital statistics
records. This comparison disclosed that 181 warrants for
OSS benefit payments had been issued to 18 clients
subsequent to their deaths. Department management
indicated these payments occurred due to the failure to
close cases and properly update computerized records as
the result of oversight by the Department's employees.
These oversights were attributed in part by the
Department to _ staff turnover. Notwithstanding this
explanation, adequate controls had not yet been
developed to provide determinations that OSS Program
benefit payments were made only to authorized clients.
Without periodic comparisons of appropriate computerized
records by Department staff, assurances that OSS
benefits are paid only to authorized clients remains
limited. Appropriate corrective actions should be enacted
to provide such comparisons. (See paragraphs 122
through 128.)

BACKGROUND

Authority

The Florida Department of Health and Rehabilitative Services was
created by Section 20.19, Florida Statutes, to provide social, rehabilitative,
and health-related services to eligible clients in need of assistance. During
the audit period, services were provided to clients through one or more of

the following programs:

Children's Medical Services provides medical care services to children

who have or are at risk for chronic, handicapping medical conditions.
Medical services are also provided to high-risk pregnant women,

sick/low birth-weight newborns, and abused children.

Economic Services administers income-support programs for needy and

indigent persons, distributes food stamps received from the Federal
Government, administers the homeless program, and_ coordinates
employment, training and supportive programs for public assistance
recipients. During the 1989-90 fiscal year the Adult Assistance Program

was transferred to Aging and Adult Services.

Developmental Services provides comprehensive services to prevent,

correct, or reduce the severity of mental retardation and other

developmental disabilities.

Aging and Adult Services advises, assists, protects, and provides

support services to the elderly, disabled adults, and other adult clients.
Income-support programs for the elderly are also provided through this

program.

Children, Youth, and Families includes adoption programs; child day

care programs; substitute and foster care programs; protective services
to children; specialized services to families; and programs related to

delinquent children.

Fees for Client Services

Section 402.33, Florida Statutes, authorizes the Department to
charge clients and/or their responsible parties fees for the cost of services
rendered to clients of the Department's various institutions and facilities.
Pursuant to this law, fees charged for the cost of care shall be reasonably
related to the cost of providing the services but shall not exceed the average
cost of the service. At least annually, the Department is required to
determine or establish the cost of providing services for which charges will
be made. Any financial benefits to which a client is entitled to by law or as
the beneficiary of a trust, annuity, retirement fund, or insurance contract
are to be used to reduce the client's or responsible party's liability for fees
assessed. In addition, Section 402.24, Florida Statutes, provides that the
Department shall automatically be subrogated to any rights an institutionalized
client has to third-party payments for medical or medically-related services
provided and/or paid for by the Department's institutions or facilities.
Accordingly, amounts recoverable from liable commercial insurance companies
and liable public institutions/programs (such as Medicare, Medicaid, and
Civilian Health and Medical Program of the Uniformed Services) shall be
collected by the Department's institutions and facilities as reimbursement for
the cost of medical or medically-related services provided eligible clients. As
part of the fee assessment and collection process, such financial benefits due

from third-parties may be assigned to the Department as representative

payee.

Section 402.33(6), Florida Statutes, provides that the Department
may not require a client or responsible party to pay fees that exceed the
client's or responsible party's ability to pay. Such ability to pay shall be
based on the client's or responsible party's gross income, number of persons
dependent on that income, and fixed domestic expenses. Accordingly, in
making these determinations, the Department is authorized to require financial
information from clients or their responsible parties. If the required
information is not furnished within the prescribed periods of time, the

Department may enter suit to enforce the requirement or may bill the client

The internal control structure established for client services could

An internal control structure for cash receipts, such as_ the

Documentation procedures used to achieve an adequate control
structure include restrictive endorsement of checks immediately upon receipt
and the preparation of records evidencing the collections immediately upon
receipt, for example, through the use of a receipt log (listing) or the
issuance of prenumbered receipts. Restrictive endorsement at the point of
receipt limits the negotiability of checks should they become lost or diverted,
while recording moneys at the point of collection facilitates accountability over
collections. Authorization procedures ensure that transactions are authorized
by management personnel acting within the scope of their authority, with the
record of such _ authorization (documentation) serving to establish
accountability for actions taken. Additionally, an adequate internal control
structure should: (1) direct that collections be remitted to an area other
than the area that initiated the billing process ; (2) safeguard the collections
until they are properly deposited; and (3) provide for a _ reconciliation
between amounts received and deposited to ensure that all collections are

deposited intact.

For the control structure described above to function properly,
there must be an adequate segregation of duties in those areas responsible
for collection activities. Certain duties should be divided among employees so
that no one employee will be in a position to complete a transaction which has
not been reviewed, approved, or recorded by other employees or units.
Generally, individuals who have access to collections should not have access
to the accounting records or reconcile the account unless there are

compensating controls.

Independent internal verification determines whether other elements
within the internal control structure are working as intended and includes
features such as comparison of records of initial receipt to evidence of
deposit. For this control element to be effective, it must not be performed
by the employees responsible for or having access to applicable assets. For

Federal regulations governing the Medicare Program establish fees

eligible clients. Generally, fees paid for physician services shall be the
lowest of the billed charge, the provider's customary charge, or the

Pursuant to Title 42, Parts 412 and 413, Code of Federal
Regulations, some providers, including the DHRS, are reimbursed by
Medicare for allowable hospital services on the reasonable cost basis. Under
this basis interim payments are made _ to providers based on _ billings
submitted. At fiscal year-end, the amounts paid by Medicare are
retroactively adjusted for the provider's actual cost of providing allowable
hospital services to Medicare eligible clients. If this actual cost is greater
than amounts paid by Medicare during the year, the provider is paid the
difference. Conversely, if the actual costs incurred are less than the

amounts paid, the provider must reimburse the Federal Government.

To determine the actual cost of providing hospital services to
Medicare eligible clients, an allocation is made of total cost incurred by the
provider in rendering of services to all clients. The amounts apportioned to
the Medicare Program are based on the ratio of Medicare beneficiary charges
to total patient charges for services rendered during the fiscal year.
Consequently, the failure to bill Medicare for allowable services rendered
during the fiscal year will result in a smaller percentage (ratio) of total
allowable costs being allocated (apportioned) to Medicare services. In turn,

this results in a retroactive adjustment less favorable to the provider.

Disposition of Funds for Deceased/Discharged Clients

Some institutions did not always dispose of client trust fund moneys

pertaining to deceased or discharged clients in accordance with controlling

laws and regulations.

Upon a client's death or discharge from an institution, any client
trust fund moneys held on his/her behalf may be claimed by and disbursed to
the client's estate or the client. Section 402.17(4), Florida Statutes,
provides that upon the death of a client, any moneys held on behalf of the
client that are not claimed should be applied first to the payment of any
unpaid claim of the State against the client (i.e., claims for cost of care),
and any balance remaining unclaimed for a period of one year shall escheat to
the State as unclaimed funds held by fiduciaries. Section 717.112, Florida
Statutes, provides that intangible property held in a fiduciary capacity for
the benefit of another person is presumed abandoned if unclaimed after seven
years. Notwithstanding Section 717.112, Florida Statutes, Department
regulations provide that the one-year period set forth in Section 402 .17(4),
Florida Statutes, controls with respect to client trust funds held under that

Section.

Optional State Supplementation Program

The Optional State Supplementation (OSS) Program was established
pursuant to Section 409.212, Florida Statutes. The purpose of the OSS
Program is to provide a supplemental income source to pay for
community-based alternative living arrangements that would prevent placement
into, or remove an individual from, an institution or nursing home. OSS
Program assistance is provided in the form of a direct cash assistance
payment to eligible individuals or their designated representatives.
Information relating to clients determined eligible for benefits is recorded in
the Department's computerized Mandatory-Optional Supplementation Record
System (MORS). The MORS initiates benefit payments for clients by means
of the payroll-by-exception method. Under this method a monthly payment is
automatically generated for each client until a subsequent change, called an
exception, is entered in the MORS to revise the client information, resulting

in an adjustment to or deletion of the monthly payment.

In paragraph 51 of audit report No. 11178, dated March 1, 1989,
we noted two instances in which the OSS Program benefits continued to be
generated and mailed to clients subsequent to their deaths. In the first
instance the client died in August 1985 but payments were not stopped until
September 1987, while in the second instance the client died in February 1987
and payments were not stopped until September 1988. We noted that,

RESPONSE TO AUDITOR GENERAL’S
PRELIMINARY AND TENTATIVE FINDINGS

We concur with the finding. HRS Accounting Procedures
Manual, Volume 11, Chapter 21, Internal Accounting
Control and Review Procedures, and Volume 11, Chapter
1, Clearing Funds and Cash Receipts, provide direction
concerning the use and reconciliation of mail-in-logs,
as well as procedures requiring the other internal
control recommendations for cash receipts. Office of
Financial Management staff, as part of its annual
internal control evaluation, will conduct a review of
all institutions prior to June 30, 1991 to ensure that
the recommended procedures are in place.

G. Pierce Wood Memorial Hospital concurs and will revise its
rates based on its required periodic cost of care :
calculation.

Headquarter’s Response

We concur with the finding that institutions should
appropriately verify the accuracy and propriety of income.
However, we only partially concur with the finding that some
institutions did not appropriately verify income through the
use of the Income Verification System (IVS) or the Third
Party Query System (TPQS), as explained in the following
paragraphs.

In some instances, the fee collection unit is not required
to verify the client’s income. For all Medicaid certified
facilities, the district Aging and Adult Services program
staff have the responsibility for verifying client income to
determine eligibility for SSI. The fee collection unit only
receives HRS-AA Form 2236, Notice of Application
Disposition, or HRS-ES Form 2233, Notice of Case Action,
which reflects the patient responsibility amount that is to
be assessed. This procedure would apply to all SSA clients
in these facilities who also are eligible for SSI benefits.

In cases where the client is employed by HRS, the
department’s own payroll records are available.

To require the use of iVS in all cases would be cost
prohibitive for the department. Currently, all IVs requests
are prepared manually and routed to the Office of Revenue
Management (ASFMR), where they are batched and forwarded to
the HRS Technology Center for keypunching. Responses are
returned to ASFMR in the form of computer printouts which
are separated and mailed to the various districts and
institutions.

The existing Accounts Receivable System design is inadequate
to properly maintain accounts on clients when detailed
transactions regarding Medicaid/Medicare billings are
involved. Numerous adjustments are required, which often
affect prior fiscal years, and cannot be handled by the
system in a way to provide an audit trail. voluminous
manual backup records are required to document the
"adjustments" that have to be made to the system.

For example, when Medicaid ICF/MR remittance vouchers
include a reduction in the payment for a prior period, some
Clients affected by the adjustment are no longer at the
facility and their accounts have been deleted from the
system. The best the facilities can do with prior
adjustments is to prorate the reduction in payment among the
current clients being paid and reduce their current payment
and collection, which is not a satisfactory solution.

Because of inadequacies in the system, the department
allowed districts and institutions the latitude of not using
the system for these complex billing situations if manual
records sufficiently documented client payments. We feel
that the department should continue this policy of
permitting manual record keeping for the Medicare/Medicaid
collections in conjunction with the accounts receivable
system. We will continue to pursue the development/purchase
of an accounts receivable software package.
