SCOPE AND OBJECTIVES

Scope

The Auditor General, as part of the Legislature’s oversight responsibility for
operations of State agencies, is responsible for reviews of electronic data processing systems.
Our review of the Florida On-Line Recipient Integrated Data Access (FLORIDA) System of the
Florida Department of Health and Rehabilitative Services, hereafter referred to as "the
Department," was conducted in accordance with GOVERNMENT AUDITING STANDARDS
issued by the Comptroller General of the United States. The proper administration of public
funds requires that management establish and maintain an internal control structure, including
the control environment, accounting system, and policies and procedures, to provide reasonable

assurance that specific entity objectives will be achieved.

Our review included an evaluation of selected control environment factors and
policies and procedures of the Department’s internal control structure relative to the FLORIDA
System during the period April 1, 1992, through October 14, 1992, and selected Department

actions taken through February 12, 1993. This included a review of:

@ The Department’s methods of acquiring computer software and systems development

services for the FLORIDA System.

@ The provisions and administration of the Department’s contract with Electronic Data Systems

Federal Corporation (EDS) for the development of the FLORIDA System.

@ The Department’s long-term plan for the implementation of the FLORIDA System and the

progress towards its accomplishment.

BACKGROUND

General

In their reviews and analyses of internal operations conducted in late 1984 and
subsequently, Department staff determined that the Department's operating environment was

characterized by two significant deficiencies. These included:

© Inefficiencies caused by multiple data processing systems in the various public assistance
and child support enforcement programs, including cumbersome or absent interfaces among

programs and systems and

e A lack of automated features in existing data processing systems which resulted in the

performance of labor intensive and error-prone activities by Department personnel.

Consequently, during 1987 and 1988, Department management, with indications of
appropriate Federal and State funding, made the decision to procure and implement a new on-line
integrated system that would eliminate the inefficiencies noted above and facilitate the provision
of services by the Department. The system purchased, termed the Florida On-Line Recipient
Integrated Data Access (FLORIDA) System, is a Statewide system that was designed to combine
information and processing functions for the Department’s Aid to Families with Dependent
Children (AFDC), Medicaid, Food Stamps, Refugee Assistance, and Child Support Enforcement
(CSE) programs, into one system. The FLORIDA System also was designed to perform the
functions formerly performed by the Department’s Income and Eligibility Verification System
(IEVS) and Overpayment, Fraud, and Recoupment Case Management System. The FLORIDA
System is functionally organized into two distinct parts: Public Assistance (PA) which consists

of the AFDC, Food Stamps, Medicaid, and Refugee Assistance programs and CSE.

International Business Machines (IBM) Corporation, Deloitte and Touche (formerly Touche Ross),

IV-D Systems, Inc., MIS Software Development, Inc. (MSD), and Florida State University.

System Description

The FLORIDA System was designed to facilitate and economize the provision of
services through the creation of a single, centralized data processing system. The System
maintains applicant/client and program information that may be electronically accessed and used
by various programs and offices. The FLORIDA System was designed to allow an applicant for
benefits to visit one of the Department’s service centers, complete one application package, and
be determined eligible or ineligible for all available programs (i.e., AFDC, Food Stamps,
Medicaid, CSE, etc.) at the same time. For those programs that provide for cash assistance to
eligible individuals, the FLORIDA System generates a computer tape that is submitted to the State

Comptroller for the generation of applicable benefit warrants.

The Department initially projected in March 1989 that the purchase and
implementation of the FLORIDA System would cost approximately $81.1 million. As of
December 31, 1992, the Department's records disclosed that approximately $104.4 million had
been expended, with the Federal Government contributing approximately 75 percent to that cost
and the State paying the remaining 25 percent. In March 1993 the Department projected that
the cost of development and implementation through March 31, 1993, would be approximately
$109 million. Because the conditions and requirements necessary to obtain final Federal
certification are currently unknown, Department staff indicated that a projection/determination

of final costs cannot currently be calculated.

The FLORIDA System’s hardware configuration consists of International Business
Machines (IBM) ES3090/600J and ES9021/900 central processing units (CPU). The System
includes data files that store recipient/client information that has been entered either on-line or
from data tapes. It uses the information on the data files to determine, among other things, the
recipients’/clients’ eligibility for benefits. As of January 21, 1993, the Department had
approximately 6,678 terminals and 1,480 personal computers (PC) distributed throughout
Department Headquarters and the || districts for accessing the FLORIDA System. Security over

access to FLORIDA System data files and programs is handled through a security software

System Implementation

The Department initially planned to implement (i.e., convert from the Department’s
former data processing systems to the FLORIDA System) the FLORIDA System during the period
May 1, 1991, through September 16, 1991. In addition, the DHHS initially planned to perform
a postinstallation certification review by February 1, 1992. To receive maximum Federal
participation in funding the System’s implementation costs, the Department must receive a
satisfactory rating after the completion of the certification review. The implementation schedule
and the related Federal postinstallation certification review have been delayed. These delays have
been attributed by the Department to program changes mandated by the Federal Family Support
Act, significant changes (i.e., increases) in the originally planned procurement activities, and the
Department having to assume all operations of the System from the contractor (i.e., EDS) earlier

than anticipated.

As of November 20, 1992, all of the Department’s districts were using the
FLORIDA System; however, not all districts had converted their entire case load to the System.
According to the Department’s records, the percentage of completion for the case load conversion
as of January 1, 1993, ranged from 88.5 percent in District XI to 100 percent in District I. In
addition, the CSE Financial Management module was not complete as of that date.
Correspondence submitted by the Department to the applicable Federal agencies in July and
August 1992 indicated that the Department plans to complete the implementation of the Public
Assistance module and be prepared for DHHS’ postinstallation certification review by March 30,

1993; and plans to complete the CSE financial management module by August 1993.

DETAILED FINDINGS AND RECOMMENDATIONS

The management of the Department is responsible for establishing and maintaining
an internal control structure. Our review of the internal control structure of the FLORIDA
System involved obtaining an understanding of the System’s control environment and control
policies and procedures. The internal control structure comprises the policies and procedures
that are designed and implemented to provide reasonable, but not absolute, assurance that specific
entity objectives will be achieved. Significant deficiencies in the design or operation of the
internal control structure could adversely affect the organization’s ability to record, process,
summarize, and report financial and programmatic data. More specifically, within an EDP
environment, a sound internal control structure provides reasonable, but not absolute, assurance
that the system’s data is complete, reliable, and free from material error or misstatement.
Because of inherent limitations in any internal control structure, errors or irregularities may
nevertheless occur and not be detected. Also, projection of any evaluation of the internal control
structure to future periods is subject to the risk that procedures may become inadequate because
of changes in conditions or that the effectiveness of the design and operation of policies and

procedures may deteriorate.

Our review of the internal control structure of the FLORIDA System indicated that
the Department has not established and maintained a control environment or control policies and
procedures to provide reasonable assurance that irregularities or material errors will be prevented
and/or detected. We listed below instances of control policies and procedures that either are not
adequately designed or not in place and control policies and procedures we recommend changing
to enhance specific areas of the internal control structure. Also, disclosed below are matters
relating to the FLORIDA System’s control environment that limited the System’s ability to
process transactions and maintain data in accordance with management’s expectations and/or
controlling laws, administrative rules, and grantor restrictions. Some of the described deficiencies
are categorized into computer aspects of the System's internal control structure. The other

deficiencies pertain to System acquisition, design, and implementation.

System Acquisition, Design, and Implementation

As noted in paragraph 11, the estimated cost to develop and implement the

FLORIDA System exceeds $100 million. Because of the System's significant cost and its vast
impact on the Department’s operations, an effective control structure was necessary to provide

reasonable assurances that:

The methods of procuring contractual services for the design and implementation of the
System were appropriate and in compliance with governing laws, administrative rules, and

grantor restrictions.

Criteria developed and used to evaluate potential contractors during the selection and award
process were fair and reasonable; and the contract evaluation and award process was

conducted on an independent and impartial basis.

Payments to contractors were in accordance with contractual terms and were only for

services rendered.

The contract for design and implementation and amendments thereto were approved by

FINDING #15

The FLORIDA System's "alert" generation process, used to notify
case workers of required actions, was not operating efficiently
as evidenced by the mass accumulation of undeleted alerts,
thereby limiting the department's assurances that its programs
were administered in accordance with controlling laws,
administrative rules, and grantor restrictions. Furthermore,
applicable user group review and approval of the department's
mass purges of the undeleted alerts was not adequately
documented, thereby limiting the department's assurance that the
correct deletions were made.

RECOMMENDATION #15A & B

The department implement measures to ensure that any subsequent
mass changes of data, such as the purge of undeleted alerts in
July and August 1992, be undertaken only after documented
approval of the planned changes by users as well as the
department's electronic data processing staff. Documentation
shoulda also be maintained to evidence approval by the affected
user groups of the results of such changes. We also recommend
that the department continue their efforts to examine the alert
generation process for the purpose of ensuring timely case
actions, while at the same time maximizing computer resources and
related response times.

MANAGEMENT’S RESPONSE #15A

We concur. Management Systems staff will implement a formal
sign-off procedure including data processing and user staff, with
appropriate retention of the sign-off documents for future mass
purges of data.

MANAGEMENT’S RESPONSE #15B

We concur. The FLORIDA Steering Committee has convened a focus
group to review the alerts generation process, and recommend
application functionality changes to reduce the overall number of
alerts. They will also make recommendations for continued
periodic purges of certain types of alerts.
