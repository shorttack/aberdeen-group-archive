PRESENTMENT

THIS MATTER came before this Grand Jury after a request for
consideration by the State Attorney's Office pursuant to our
authority to conduct investigations of public institutions and
personnel, including practices or procedures regarding public
offices, public officials and public monies. The request was to
look into practices, procedures, expenditure of money, conduct of
public officials and public employees regarding The Florida
Department of Health and Rehabilitative Services (HRS),
Management Systems, the Florida On-Line Recipient Integrated Data
Access (FLORIDA) System, and other projects dnd matters. Also,
this review involved any possible criminal misconduct by public
officials and employees and others. Our review of this matter
included sworn testimony of material witnesses, review-of various
forms of evidence presented and field trips to The Florida
Department of Health and Rehabilitative Services (HRS) -
Management Systems and to a meeting of the Information Technology

Resource Procurement Advisory Council.

We find evidence of the following factual sequence based

1.

sworn testimony of material witnesses and evidence

Prior to 1987, The Florida Department of Health and

Rehabilitative Services (HRS) had mainly Burroughs computers and


<!-- [p. 3] -->
**[p. 3]**

mainframes. Burroughs later became Unisys. The Data Center was
located in Jacksonville, Florida.

2. During 1986, The Florida Department of Health and
Rehabilitative Services (HRS) had three systems shut down by the
Federal Government. The systems were FAMIS (Welfare and Food
Stamps), Child Support and Medicaid. The State had been
unsuccessful in obtaining maximum federal funding. Everything
was unacceptable with the Federal Government. The State needed
an automated system that would cut down the error rate.

3. When Governor Bob Martinez was elected, Mr. Greg Coler
was eventually appointed Secretary of The Florida Department of
Health and Rehabilitative Services (HRS).

4. Mr. John Lyons, who was employed by International
Business Machines Corporation (IBM) beginning in January, 1987,
established contact with Secretary Greg Coler. Secretary Coler
asked Mr. Lyons to check out the Department's Data Center in
Jacksonville. Upon completion of his review, Mr. Lyons drafted
an Executive Presentation on how IBM could improve the statewide
computer system of the Department (HRS). His plan would improve
the statewide telecommunications network of the Department. The
plan called for a hook up (PC MUCKS). wherein IBM computers could
communicate with Unisys computers. This would be a step in the
direction of establishing an IBM environment within the
Department (HRS).

5. Under the leadership of Secretary Coler new managers

appeared in 1987 and they had an IBM orientation. Among these


<!-- [p. 4] -->
**[p. 4]**

new managers was Ms. Viann Hardy who would later become Deputy
Secretary in charge of Management Systems at the Department
(HRS).

6. Mr. Tom Johnson, who would later move up the ranks to
Assistant Deputy Secretary under Deputy Secretary Viann Hardy,
was hired by HRS with the help of IBM. IBM definitely influenced
and supported the hiring of Mr. Johnson. During his employment
Mr. Johnson made it clear to the Department's (HRS) employees
that all he wanted was IBM hardware.

7. A decision was made to roll the three aforementioned
unsuccessful projects together and the new project would be
called the Florida On-Line Recipient Integrated Data Access
System (The Florida System). This occurred in 1987 and 1988.
This system would be divided into two distinct parts: (1) Public
Assistance (PA) which consists of AFDC, Food Stamps, Medicaid and
Refugee Assistance Programs and (2) Child Support Enforcement
(CSE).

8. The Department of Health and Rehabilitative Services
(HRS) procured the services of a consultant (MAXIMUS) to prepare
a Request for Proposal (RFP) and applicable planning documents.

9. During the months of April and May of 1988, approval of
the RFP was received from the State Information Technology
Resource Procurement Advisory Council (ITRPAC), the United States
Department of Agriculture's (USDA) Food and Nutrition Service
(FNS) and the United States Department of Health and Human

Services (DHHS).

10. The Florida On-Line Recipient Integrated Data Access
System was to (a) improve services to clients in the form of
accurate eligibility determinations and timely payments, (b)
improve operational efficiency which would reduce program errors
and achieve savings, and (c) reduce paperwork and eliminate
redundant and unnecessary tasks thus reducing cost.

ll. The Request for Proposal (RFP) for the Florida On-Line
Recipient Integrated Data Access System (Florida System) was
officially advertised and issued to prospective vendors on May
20, 1988. The Department (HRS) received proposals from CSxX
Technology, Electronic Data Systems Federal Corporation (EDS) and
Unisys Corporation. There was a twenty-six (26) month schedule
in the RFP to bring the Florida System on-line.

12. Unisys Corporation filed a protest stating that the
twenty-six (26) month schedule was unrealistic, the RFP was vague
and the RFP did not allow the use of existing equipment. This
protest was filed within three days after the RFP was released.
Also there was a claim made that the twenty-six (26) months was
based on politics and was designed to unfold while Bob Martinez
was Governor. A hearing officer ruled against Unisys except for
the proposed time period. The hearing extended the time period
to twenty-nine (29) months. The RFP was modified to comply with
the hearing officer's ruling. During these twenty-nine (29)
months the State of Florida would receive enhanced funding for
the project.

the Florida On-Line Recipient Integrated Data Access System (The
Florida System).

14. The Evaluation Team was to review and evaluate the
different sections of the proposal. Individuals were assigned
different areas to review. Each member was instructed not to
discuss their evaluation with anyone except when instructed to do
so by the coordinator. The findings of the Evaluation Team go to
the Selection Team who makes the recommendation to the Secretary
of the Department (HRS).

15. Ms. Martha Crabb, a member of the Evaluation Team, was
contacted by then Assistant Secretary Don Winstead. Assistant
Secretary Winstead stated, in essence, that EDS was the best
system and if she thought EDS was the best, then she should score
the way she.felt. Ms. Crabb was concerned about this contact and
felt that it was unethical. Mr. Winstead held a high position
within the Department (HRS) and was also a member of the
Selection Team.

16. Ms. Karen Girard, a member of the Evaluation Team, was
contacted by other members of the Evaluation Team, Mr. Tom
Johnson and Ms. Lou Gettis, in a parking lot, and was advised by
them that EDS was the best vendor. Based on what Mr. Johnson and
Ms. Gettis told her she changed her vote from CSX to EDS. She
felt that what Mr. Johnson and Ms. Gettis did was unethical and
violated the rules and procedures of the evaluation process. Mr.

Johnson and Ms. Gettis had positions of authority within the

available under applicable federal law.

CONCLUSION

The Grand Jury sincerely hopes that this Presentment and the
recommendations contained herein will not be received with rancor
or hostility but as an honest effort to inspire official action
which will avoid future costly mistakes and instill public
confidence and increased accountability in the operation of our
public institutions. The Grand Jury believes that if any good is
to come out of this deplorable affair, it can only be through
substantial changes in the attitudes and performance of public
officials and the laws and rules that control their power,
authority, and responsibility. The Grand Jury commends those HRS
employees who courageously disclosed the mismanagement of the
FLORIDA System despite the coercive managerial environment that
prevailed.
